# Comprehensive, source-led curriculum expansion

## Goal
Make all 153 curriculum topics and their teaching subsections genuinely detailed, not collections of short outline bullets. Include anatomy and chemistry as well as physics, physiology, pharmacology, clinical anaesthesia, intensive care and perioperative care.

Use the expanded APRV section as an example of depth and structure, not as a rigid template or word-count target.

## Required depth
For each subject, cover the relevant elements:
- Definition, underlying mechanisms and why they matter.
- Detailed explanation of the main concepts, relationships and practical interpretation.
- Clinical or equipment applications, decision-making and alternatives.
- Safety, contraindications, complications, monitoring and troubleshooting where applicable.
- Evidence, uncertainties and limitations, clearly separated from established knowledge.
- Worked examples, calculations, diagram explanations, model viva answers and quizzes where they add value.

Summary boxes should remain concise, but link to detailed teaching rather than being the only explanation.

## Source-first safeguards
- Verify the original source before writing new recommendations, numerical thresholds or doses.
- Use reputable textbooks, primary studies and current official guidance; check bibliographic details and whether each source actually supports its attached claim.
- Show citations beside the relevant teaching. Add short verbatim excerpts for important numerical recommendations when the source is accessible.
- Never invent quotes, citations or claims of clinical certification. Flag inaccessible or conflicting evidence explicitly.
- Preserve sound existing material; correct unsupported claims and explain material changes rather than silently deleting them.

## Delivery
1. Review the actual topic pages and subsections, including diagrams and shared teaching blocks. Do not infer completeness from file size or the shortened audit text.
2. Build a topic/subsection checklist of missing depth and questionable source support. Prioritise thin, high-risk clinical teaching, then cover every remaining discipline.
3. Expand in bounded batches of approximately 5–8 topics, smaller when source verification is extensive. Each batch is an implemented, checked result; report exactly what was expanded and what remains.
4. Keep existing page organisation, exam filters and mobile readability. Avoid duplicating detailed content across related topics; cross-link the authoritative explanation.
5. Finish with a curriculum-wide coverage review. A topic is not complete merely because it has a reference list, a quiz or a long page.

The whole curriculum will require multiple batches; approval starts the first implementation batch, not an unattended promise to finish later.

## Technical verification
- Keep topic teaching in the existing TopicTemplate structure and existing section/exam mappings.
- Add regression checks for the expanded material, reference-label resolution and numerical worked examples where appropriate.
- Check current build results and exercise the changed pages, diagrams and quizzes in the browser at desktop and phone sizes.
- Rebuild and upload the private audit corpus after each batch. Verify that the added prose, shared teaching and diagram labels are actually retained rather than silently truncated.
- Maintain the batch checklist in roadmap.md; do not mark unverified content or unrelated existing audit findings as resolved.

## Completion standard
All topics reviewed at subsection level; identified outline-only gaps expanded; material recommendations tied to supporting sources; changed learning controls and diagrams checked; remaining source-access or specialist-review limitations explicitly listed.
